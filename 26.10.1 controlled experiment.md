# 26.10.1 controlled experiment — E3 learning-rate schedule

## Question and controls

Does E1-A's longer learning-rate reduction patience improve training when the model can continue beyond 2,000 updates? E3 changes the scheduler reduction patience from 200 to 500 optimizer updates (`ReduceLROnPlateau` patience from 1 to 4 validation checks). Its absolute improvement threshold remains `0.001`; the reduction factor remains `0.5`. All other model, optimizer, data, evaluation, and fixed-budget settings match E2's 10,000-update runs.

Five paired seeds were used: `42`, `1006`, `10086`, `104792`, and `230786`. E3 ran each seed continuously to 10,000 updates. E2 values below come from the matching seed's **10,000-update continuous run**, evaluated at the same checkpoints. Train and validation dataset hashes match between E2 and E3 for every seed. The reserved test split was not evaluated.

## Five-seed means at fixed updates

All MSE values are validation standardized MSE for the `model` predictor at the stated update. MAE uses physical units. The intermediate pass rate requires displacement error `< 0.01 m` **and** velocity error `< 0.01 m/s` on the same row.

At 10,000 updates, E3's mean validation MSE was `0.002026782` lower, a `36.6%` reduction relative to E2. All five paired seeds improved in MSE. Mean training MSE also fell from `0.005571031` to `0.003557779`. Physical MAE improved, but the intermediate pass rate remained very low: `0.16%` means an average of 1.6 passing rows per 1,000-row validation split.

## Learning-rate behavior

E2 first reduced its learning rate from `0.001` to `0.0005` at updates `1,400`, `1,800`, `1,800`, `1,900`, and `2,100` across the five seeds. E3 first reduced it at `2,900`, `2,500`, `3,300`, `2,500`, and `2,700`, respectively. Thus the schedule change affected training after the early checkpoints. The E3 result supports retaining the higher initial learning rate longer for this setup, while still showing that lower MSE alone does not meet the strict joint tolerance goal.
